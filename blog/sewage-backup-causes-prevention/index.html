Why Sewage Backups Happen in Mansfield, TX

Home / Blog / Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Mansfield, TX

For a homeowner, a backed-up sewer line ranks near the top of the list of unpleasant emergencies. The result is extensive damage, an immediate threat to health, and a professional cleanup bill that can reach thousands of dollars whenever raw sewage enters the rooms you live in. Your risk drops significantly when you know what causes backups and take steps to prevent them. Below, Mansfield Restoration Co explains what usually causes them for Mansfield, TX homeowners, plus the practical things you can do to protect your home.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

Sewage backups occur when wastewater cannot flow from your property to the municipal sewer system or septic tank as intended. Most cases trace back to the following: sewer pipes that have aged or collapsed, roots from trees intruding into the pipe, grease, hair, and debris clogging the line, heavy rainfall overloading the municipal sewers, a combined sewer overflow event, or improper items going down the toilet, including excessive paper, wipes, and feminine products.

How Tree Roots Block Sewer Pipes

In Mansfield, many blocked sewer lines turn out to be the work of tree roots. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. Entry happens at connection points, cracks, and joints, and over time the growth packs the pipe, creating the blockages behind many backups. Older clay tile sewer pipes are particularly vulnerable to root intrusion. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

Ways to Lower Your Backup Risk

To guard a Mansfield house against backups, start by having a backwater valve fitted to your sewer connection so the city system cannot flow back in. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Only flush toilet paper, never wipes, feminine products, or other items. Have the sewer line inspected and cleaned on a regular schedule. A drain that is running slow needs prompt attention, or it may block completely. In an emergency someone will need the sewer cleanout, so learn where yours sits. Consider sewer line insurance or a sewage backup endorsement on your homeowner's policy.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
